# AZURE — delivery report

Build verified on 23 November 2026 against Next.js 16.4.6 / React 19.3.1. Every number below comes from commands actually run on the delivered code; caveats are stated where the build environment limited what could be verified.

## Approved premium design (revision, 23 November 2026)

The client reviewed design directions on a design canvas and approved a photograph-led luxury design modelled on a reference they supplied. The site now follows it on every page:

- **Palette (sampled from the client's reference image):** deep forest green (`forest` #1D2218, darkest `night` #121209) for immersive sections, warm cream grounds (`cream` #F8F0E8, `linen` #F3EBE1), a sand accent for buttons on green (`sand` #E0D1B9), near-black type and taupe (#5B5144) for prices and notes, parchment (#D5CEB6) labels on green. Photography is never darkened by overlays: hero photographs dissolve into the green at their left and top edges with a CSS mask instead.
- **Typography:** Cormorant Garamond headings in title case with an italic accent word, Manrope for interface text, spaced capitals with a hairline rule for section labels.
- **Layout:** dark heroes on every page; edge-to-edge photo/text splits; signature dishes as cards closed by a dark "view full menu" tile; a gallery strip; a "Your Table Awaits" band; an ivory footer.
- **Photography:** real, dark and candle-lit food photography in the spirit of the reference (a peppered beef fillet with a candle and red wine for the hero, scallops with caviar, truffle shaved over tagliatelle, herb-crusted lamb, a candle-lit table, a warm dining room). The photos in the reference itself are a low-resolution mockup of unknown origin, so each was matched with a licensed Unsplash photograph instead. Every new photograph was checked full-size in a real browser; two candidates were rejected because they showed another business's branding (a beer label, a restaurant's printed menu). The signature dishes on the menu (pan-seared scallops, truffle tagliatelle, herb-crusted lamb) were written to match their photographs.

### Motion

- **Opening sequence** (first visit per session, about five seconds): a hairline opens into a small window in the dark and the courses arrive in it one after another (scallops with caviar, truffle shaved over tagliatelle, lamb shank, then the beef fillet) behind the AZURE wordmark, while a hairline and counter show real loading progress. The name then lifts away and the window opens out until the fillet lands exactly where the hero shows it; the page fades up around it, so the hand-off has no cut or curtain. Skippable; absent with reduced motion; never waits more than about 4.4 s for loading.
- **Hero slideshow:** three photographs crossfade with a slow zoom and a progress line under the active number; pausable (WCAG 2.2.2), pauses on hover and in background tabs, off with reduced motion. The headline rises line by line as the intro's curtains lift.
- **Scroll:** inertial wheel scrolling (Lenis, synced to GSAP's ticker; native on touch devices); photographs unmask upwards and drift with subtle parallax; headings rise line by line; label rules grow in; botanical line drawings draw their strokes as they arrive.
- **Interaction:** photographs ease in on hover, arrows nudge forward, nav underlines draw in, gallery captions slide up, the page-change curtain shows the monogram and wordmark.
- **3D:** "The pairing" section shows a live crystal glass of white wine on a dark oak table, with the dining room's candle-light out of focus behind it. The camera drifts around the glass as the section scrolls; moving the pointer turns the light across the crystal and tilts the glass a breath. A pre-rendered still of the same scene stands in for devices that cannot run WebGL.

## 2. Interactions

- Navigation: a transparent header over the dark hero that turns forest green on scroll and tucks away while reading (not on the menu page); a Framer Motion active-page indicator; drawn underlines on hover/focus; a skip link; focus moves to `<main>` after navigation.
- Mobile menu: a full-screen Floating UI dialog with staggered links, focus trap, Escape and outside-press to close, focus return and scroll lock.
- Page transitions: a ~0.9 s forest-green curtain on internal links. Skipped for modifier-clicks, same-page hashes, back/forward and reduced motion.
- Menu: category and diet filters (all diets must match), Framer Motion layout transitions, an empty state with reset, a live result count for screen readers, shareable `?category=&diet=` URLs, Floating UI tooltips on diet marks (hover and keyboard focus), and an accessible dish dialog.
- Gallery: lightbox with previous/next, ← → keys, Escape, a live counter and focus return.
- Reservations: guest stepper (1–8), Floating UI date-picker popover with a keyboard-navigable calendar (arrows, Page Up/Down, Home/End; closed and out-of-window days are announced), live availability from `/api/availability` with loading, error/retry and "full" states, details with inline validation and an error summary linked to fields, a loading state, and a confirmation with a `DEMO-` reference. All of it runs through a Server Action that re-validates on the server.
- Every interactive element has hover, focus-visible, active (press) and, where relevant, disabled, loading, error and success states.

## 3. Three.js

- A crystal wine glass turned on a lathe from a measured profile (foot, stem, bowl and a rounded rim with real wall thickness), in drei's `MeshTransmissionMaterial` (refraction with back faces, slight chromatic aberration). The white wine is its own volume, tinted deeper gold towards its edges, with the crystal's reflections redrawn over it.
- Lighting: drei `Lightformer` strip lights and a softbox plus the room itself, rendered once into the environment map (no HDR download). The room behind the glass is a painted texture of out-of-focus candle and lamp light that melts into the section's green, so the stage has no visible frame. The table is a blurred mirror (`MeshReflectorMaterial`) on desktop and a satin surface elsewhere; the glass's shadow and caustic are painted, not computed.
- Scroll-coupled camera drift (GSAP ScrollTrigger → R3F `useFrame`), pointer-driven light rotation and glass tilt (mouse only; nothing depends on hover on touch).
- Progressive: a rendered still is the baseline. The ~264 KB-gzip Three.js chunk loads only within 600 px of the viewport and only on capable devices. Rendering stops off-screen. `PerformanceMonitor` adapts DPR and falls back on sustained low FPS. WebGL context loss and runtime errors fall back to the still. GPU resources are disposed on unmount.
- Tiers: high (desktop: 8 refraction samples at 1024 px, mirror table, DPR ≤ 2, MSAA), medium (tablets/capable phones: 6 samples at 512 px, satin table, DPR ≤ 1.5), static (no WebGL / software rendering / reduced motion / Save-Data / low memory or cores / modest phones).

## 4. Animation

- GSAP: the opening sequence (a window of courses behind the wordmark that opens out into the hero photograph, a real loading counter that never waits more than about 4.4 s, a skip button, once per session, absent with reduced motion and graceful without JS); the hero entrance and scroll-out; SplitText line reveals; image mask reveals; parallax; scroll-linked display type.
- Framer Motion: the page curtain, mobile menu, dialogs, lightbox, filter layout, nav indicator, tooltips, popover and confirmation.
- Rules: nothing loops except the hero slideshow (pausable) and the wine's faint sway in the 3D scene (which stops off-screen). There is no scroll hijacking, no cursor followers and no bounce. Reveals use opacity, so unrevealed content stays in the accessibility tree. `prefers-reduced-motion` disables the intro, reveals, parallax, the curtain and live 3D.

## 5. Responsive coverage

Verified automatically (no horizontal overflow, every header element inside the viewport) on all four pages and the 404 at **320, 375, 430, 600, 768, 1024, 1280, 1600, 1920, 2560 and 3840 px**. Reading measure stays under 1100 px on a 4K screen. Layouts use a 4 → 8 → 12-column grid, `clamp()` type and spacing, and a 1600 px content cap. Full-page screenshots were reviewed at 375, 768, 1440 and 1920 px. That review found and fixed: the header CTA overflowing at 375 px (a class conflict), the tasting image overflowing by 73 px, background photos with zero height, gallery dead space, and footer email breaking.

- axe-core (WCAG 2.0/2.1/2.2 A + AA): **0 violations** on all four pages, the 404, and an open dish dialog.
- Lighthouse Accessibility: **100** on all pages, mobile and desktop.
- Semantic landmarks, one `h1` per page, labelled forms with linked error messages, `aria-invalid`, an error summary, live regions for async results, Floating UI dialogs with focus management, keyboard-operable calendar, gallery, tooltips and filters, visible focus rings, ≥ 44 px touch targets, palette contrast checked (Slate Ink on Porcelain ≈ 10:1; Sapphire is used only on Navy), and text alternatives for the 3D scene (the scene is never the only source of information).

**Trade-off, stated plainly:** the home page on a first visit plays the opening sequence and the hero slideshow, which costs main-thread time on throttled mobile (scores in the high 80s instead of the high 90s the previous, motion-light design reached). ## 10. Known limitations and remaining work

1. **Photography** is unreviewed stock (see §6). Commission and replace it before any client presentation of a real venue.
2. **Business facts** (address, phone, social accounts, real hours) are placeholders by design. Fill `src/content/site.ts`. `NEXT_PUBLIC_MAP_EMBED_URL` is optional: set it to swap the drawn harbour map for a real map embed.
3. **Bookings are demonstrations.** Connect a real system with `RESERVATION_WEBHOOK_URL`/`_SECRET` or a provider adapter (`src/lib/reservations/provider.ts`). Availability is simulated (`simulatedOccupancy`).
4. **CSP allows inline scripts** (a static-rendering trade-off, documented in `docs/SECURITY.md` with the nonce migration path).
5. **Rate limits are per instance** unless Upstash is configured.
6. **Vercel Hobby is non-commercial.** Use a commercial-friendly free tier or a paid plan for a real restaurant.
7. **Lighthouse ran without photos** and on local hardware. Confirm with CI/field data after deployment.
8. CI workflows are written but have not run on GitHub yet (no remote repository exists).
9. Not built (out of scope or needing the owner): CMS integration, multilingual content, analytics vendor, cookie consent (no cookies or third-party trackers are used today), gift vouchers and private-dining enquiry forms.
